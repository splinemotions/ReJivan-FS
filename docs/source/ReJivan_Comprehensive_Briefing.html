1. Project Vision & Competition Grounding

ReJivan is an automated healthcare coordination and continuous safety monitoring platform built for Hack for Social Cause (HSC 2027), part of VBYLD 2027 organized by the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports (MoYAS) with IIT Bombay as Knowledge Partner (Official Submission Deadline: 15 October 2026; National Showcase: 10–12 January 2027 in New Delhi).

The Problem in Andaman & Nicobar Islands (UT): The Andaman & Nicobar archipelago spans 36 inhabited islands with only one major tertiary referral hospital (GB Pant Hospital in Port Blair). Remote Community Health Centers and PHCs on outer islands (such as Hut Bay, Little Andaman) face severe weather disruptions, ferry delays, and zero round-the-clock intensive care specialists. Families are frequently divided between mainland work and elderly relatives left alone on the islands.

The ReJivan Solution: A dual-layer monitoring architecture that combines medical wearables (vital signs: HR, SpO2, BP, Temperature, Glucose) with privacy-first camera zones (posture, gait instability, falls, shivering/tremors, and prolonged immobility) coupled with an automatic 3-tier emergency escalation ladder.

3. Hardware Realities & Constraints

Developer Machine Specifications

  • GPU: NVIDIA CUDA-class GPU, auto-detected at runtime (current development machine: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU, 8GB VRAM).
  • Inference Capacity: Capable of running lightweight pose models such as yolov8n-pose.pt or yolo11n-pose.pt (~150MB VRAM footprint) at 25–60 FPS locally using PyTorch/CUDA or ONNX Runtime, with automatic CPU fallback on machines without a CUDA GPU.
  • Client Deployment Environment: Public production deployed on Vercel Serverless (Node.js runtime, ephemeral execution, 10–15 second function timeouts, zero GPU).

4. Crucial Clarifications & Architectural Decisions

A. Solving the "Phone Drop" False Alarm (Resident Verification Loop)

The Problem: If an elderly person has their phone in their pocket or on a bedside table, and the phone slips or falls to the floor, an accelerometer impact spike will occur. If the system treated accelerometer spikes alone as a human medical emergency, it would cause intolerable false alarms.

The ReJivan Solution (15–30s Verification Prompt): 

Accelerometer Impact Spike (>2.5g)
            │
            ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  PHONE VIBRATES & AUDIO CHIME SOUNDS   │
│  Modal: "Did you fall or drop device?" │
│  Countdown: 30s · Large Buttons        │
└──────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                   │
         ┌─────────┴─────────┐
         ▼                   ▼
["I'm Okay / Dropped"]   [No Response after 30s OR "Need Help"]
         │                   │
         ▼                   ▼
  Event Cancelled        🚨 Trigger 3-Tier Emergency Ladder
(Logged as false alarm)   (Family → Backup → 108 Ambulance)

Dual-Mode App Design:

  • Mode 1 (Virtual Ward / Bedside Monitor): For bedridden patients in hospitals or at home. The patient does not handle the device. Nursing staff and family use the app to observe live telemetry, camera zones, and medication schedules.
  • Mode 2 (Personal SOS Guardian Mode): For ambulatory, independent seniors living alone. The app runs in background pocket mode; motion shocks trigger the verification prompt before calling emergency contacts.

B. Solving the "Portability" Dilemma (In-Browser Vision vs. Edge AI)

The Dilemma: If 30 FPS YOLO vision inference cannot run directly on Vercel serverless cloud, how does ReJivan maintain 100% portability so anyone with any laptop or phone can test it anywhere?

The Two-Tier Solution:

  1. Tier 1: Client-Side In-Browser Computer Vision (100% Portable): Using MediaPipe Pose / ONNX Runtime Web / WebAssembly (Wasm) compiled directly into JavaScript in the browser. When an evaluator or judge visits https://rejivan2.vercel.app, the browser itself accesses the webcam or preloaded video and tracks the 33 body landmarks locally in real time with zero server GPU required. This is completely portable, free, and respects privacy.
  2. Tier 2: Hospital Edge Box (High-Performance Clinical Deployment): In a dedicated hospital installation (e.g. GB Pant Hospital Ward A), a local PC with a CUDA-capable NVIDIA GPU runs a Python background worker with YOLO-Pose + OpenCV. The local machine processes raw RTSP video, generates structured JSON event telemetry, and pushes only lightweight events to the ReJivan cloud portal.

5. Proposed Advanced Architecture: Multimodal Event Causality

Based on expert feedback, ReJivan is evolving from a simple "Fall Detector" into a Contextual Physical Event Reconstruction Engine.

Core Differentiator: CCTV + Wearable Multimodal Fusion Matrix

CCTV Observable Signal Wearable / Sensor Signal System Physical Reasoning Action / Alert Tier
Stationary on bed / chair Normal vitals (HR 72, SpO2 98%) Normal resting or sleep state Level 0: Normal (No alert)
Walking with slight lateral sway Normal vitals, normal step cadence Mild gait irregularity or fatigue Level 1: Anomaly (Log & observe)
Repetitive wrist/arm oscillation (3-6 Hz) Accelerometer confirms oscillation Unusual tremor or shivering pattern Level 2: Concerning (Prompt & record)
Rapid downward displacement → floor Zero impact shock, controlled descent Intentional kneeling, sitting, or resting Level 1: Monitor (Check in 60s)
Rapid downward displacement → floor Impact shock (>2.8g) + no recovery motion True Fall Incident (Trip / Loss of balance) Level 2: Verify (30s prompt → Level 3)
Motionless on floor (>45 seconds) Abnormal vitals (HR spike / SpO2 drop) Acute Medical Emergency / Incapacitation Level 3: Critical (Instant 108 dispatch)
Motionless on floor (>45 seconds) Wearable signal LOST / Disconnected Sensor connectivity dropout (Unknown state) Warning (Check sensor connection)

The Pre-Incident Causality Timeline

Instead of screaming "FALL DETECTED", ReJivan presents an explainable 30-second historical sequence: 

[14:31:42]  Walking normally · Velocity 0.84 m/s · Step symmetry 96%
[14:31:48]  Gait velocity decreases → 0.42 m/s · Lateral torso sway detected
[14:31:51]  Sudden vertical displacement (Δy: -1.8m/s) → Ground posture
[14:31:52]  Impact confirmed by sensor telemetry (3.4g peak shock)
[14:31:55]  Zero recovery movement detected for 15 seconds

[14:31:57]  Counterfactual: Intentional descent ruled out due to high-impact deceleration
[14:31:58]  Interactive Resident Verification initiated (30s timer active)
